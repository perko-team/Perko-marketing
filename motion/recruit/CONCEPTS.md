# Concepts

## 1. Expected: "The empty slot"
The real Perko wallet stack on a light ground; cards slide in one by one (sample businesses), then one slot stays empty with a dashed outline: "the spot for your business". Money shot: the wallet with the glowing empty slot. Wow: the empty slot pulls the camera into it and the frame becomes the join card. Transition: the card stack fans into the end card. Fits: shows the real product. Risk: reads as a product demo, not a call to the neighbourhood.

## 2. Bold: "The street fills in"
A paper model of Emek Refaim and Derech Beit Lehem (stylised card-stock blocks, not real storefronts) seen low along the street; every door carries an empty stamp ring. Money shot: overhead, the two streets as one lime line with a row of empty rings. Wow: on the drop the camera cranes from street level to overhead in one move and the rings link into a single route: one network, still waiting for its first stamps. Transition: the route becomes the underline of the CTA. Fits: local, honest (the rings are empty on purpose). Risk: a map can read as an infographic if the camera is timid.

## 3. Unexpected: "Through the punch hole"
A giant paper punch card fills the frame, macro on the card fibres. A hole punches out on the first beat and the camera flies through it: on the other side, the paper street (from 2) seen through a circular window. Each remaining hole on the card is a door still waiting. Money shot: the circle of the punched hole framing the street, lime light coming through. Wow: on the drop, the card's holes punch out one after another down the street in a single sweep, and the card folds into the phone wallet. Transition: the punched hole is the logo's disc on the end card. Fits: everyone knows the paper punch card Perko replaces; the hole literally opens onto the neighbourhood. Risk: the hardest to build; needs strong paper materials and a precise camera.

**Recommendation:** 3, with the street from 2 behind the hole. It is the boldest, and the message still reads in one watch: the old card opens a door to the street, the street is waiting for its first businesses.
